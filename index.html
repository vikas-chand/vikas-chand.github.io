Postdoctoral Researcher · Louisiana State University

High-energy transients of the universe.

I study gamma-ray bursts and magnetar giant flares — the most energetic explosions we can observe — using Fermi-LAT, AstroSat-CZTI, and multi-wavelength data to test the physics of relativistic outflows.

01

Bio

Gamma-ray bursts release in seconds what the Sun emits over its entire ten-billion-year lifetime. My work asks how, and where, the energy comes from.

I'm a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Physics & Astronomy at Louisiana State University. Previously I held postdoctoral positions at the Astrophysics Research Center of the Open University (ARCO) in Israel, with Profs. Jonathan Granot and Paz Beniamini, and at Nanjing University in China, with Binbin Zhang.

I earned my PhD in physics from the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), Mumbai, with a thesis on the spectro-polarimetry of gamma-ray bursts under Prof. A. R. Rao. My research combines Fermi-LAT data analysis, time-resolved spectroscopy, and physics-driven modelling to interrogate the emission mechanisms of gamma-ray bursts and magnetar giant flares.

I'm currently looking for tenure-track faculty positions where I can build a group at the intersection of multi-messenger astronomy and time-domain high-energy astrophysics.

Research

Figure — e.g. GRB 200415A LAT light curve
Magnetar giant flares

Transient GeV emission from MGF candidates

Discovery and characterisation of high-energy gamma-ray emission following magnetar giant flares — extending what we know about extragalactic short bursts and the physics of neutron-star starquakes.

Objects — GRB 200415A · Fermi-LAT
Figure — e.g. broadband SED / SSC model
Very-high-energy GRBs

Sub-GeV cutoffs and TeV emission

Time-resolved spectral modelling of bright LAT bursts — connecting prompt-phase cutoffs to afterglow synchrotron-self-Compton signatures observed at TeV energies by MAGIC and H.E.S.S.

Objects — GRB 190114C · GRB 190829A
Figure — e.g. polarization angle vs. time
Polarimetry

Variable prompt-emission polarisation

Time-dependent polarisation analysis of the prompt phase using AstroSat-CZTI — direct constraints on jet geometry and the magnetic-field structure of GRB outflows, including violations of the synchrotron line of death.

Objects — GRB 171010A · GRB 160802A
Figure — e.g. time-resolved νFν spectra
Emission mechanisms

Synchrotron vs. photospheric models

Physics-driven spectral fitting of the prompt phase to discriminate between synchrotron, Comptonisation, and photospheric origin — including fast-cooling internal-shock models for bursts like GRB 241030A.

Objects — Time-resolved · Internal shocks
